# Warm Cartography
*The illustration system for Mischa Wright Insurance Agency*

## The movement
Warm Cartography treats a family's future as terrain: something that can be mapped, walked, and prepared for. Every composition is a landscape at the quiet hour — layered hills, a steady horizon, a low warm sun. Nothing dramatic ever happens in these images. That is the point. Insurance planning done well is the removal of drama, and the pictures say so before the copy does.

## Space and form
Deep layered silhouettes recede toward a luminous horizon. Foregrounds are dark and certain (navy), midgrounds soften through translucency, and the horizon always glows (cream into gold). The eye travels the same journey the visitor is asked to take: from where they stand now toward a calmer place further on. Forms are rounded, geologic, unhurried. A small human trace appears in each scene — a house, a path, a bridge, a lighthouse — never a face, so every viewer can place their own family inside the frame.

## Color and material
The site's exact palette, and only it: navy #12364A and its deeper register #0B2737 carry the land; rust #A65432 appears once per composition as the sun or a single warm accent, the way rust is used on the site itself — sparingly, meaningfully; cream #F5EFE5 through gold #E8C79A holds the sky. A fine photographic grain (2–3% opacity) sits over everything, giving the flat vector forms the warmth of printed lithography. The result should read as silkscreen posters commissioned from a careful studio, meticulously crafted, labored over, the product of deep expertise — never as generated decoration.

## Scale and rhythm
Horizons sit low or at the golden section, never centered. Hills repeat in odd counts. The sun is small relative to the sky — hope is present but not shouting. Repetition builds meaning: the same sun, the same grain, the same layering logic across all thirteen images, so the whole site feels like one atlas drawn by one hand over many patient hours.

## The signature
Every piece carries the same low rust sun. Someone who has seen one image recognizes the next. That consistency is the craftsmanship: a system, not a set of stock pictures.
